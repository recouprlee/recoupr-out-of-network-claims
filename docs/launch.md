# Your superbill should be the start, not the end

By Lee Knight

## What a superbill is

A superbill is an itemized record of care from a therapist. It can show the therapist and practice details, dates of service, service codes, diagnosis information, charges, and what the client paid. It gives an insurer many of the facts needed to review an out-of-network claim. It is not usually the finished claim.

What happens next can feel like a second job. The client receives a PDF or a sheet of paper and is left to find the right member claim form, understand the requested fields, gather proof of payment, choose a submission route, and keep a record of what was sent. If the claim is denied, the client must read the EOB or denial letter, find the reason, and prepare the next response. A superbill may be complete and still leave the client at the starting line.

## What the skill does

recoupr Out-of-Network Claims reads the superbill and the plan pages a person chooses to share. It checks the source facts without changing them. It can prepare a claim field sheet, cover letter, attachment list, and tracker. It can explain supplied out-of-network benefits and do the stated arithmetic when every input is present. If there is a denial, it can prepare an appeal for review. The person reviews the result and submits it. recoupr does not receive the documents.

## What it will never do

The skill will never file a claim or appeal. It will not log in, send an email, make a call, or contact an insurer or therapist. It will not change a code, date, fee, diagnosis, provider detail, plan fact, or insurer rule. It leaves signatures and assignment fields for the named person. It does not promise coverage, payment, or acceptance. When a source is missing or conflicts with another source, it says so. The insurer's current page and the person's plan control.

## What was read

The skill was built from 76 insurers' public member claim pages and forms. The research was read in October 2026. The local form map contains 77 form files, plus its index. One insurer has an additional regional form, which is why the mapped form count is one higher than the insurer count.

Each insurer row records its source URL and read date. Missing public details stay marked as not published. The copy does not fill those gaps from memory. Routes and forms can change, so every prepared result points the person back to the insurer's own page before submission. That page controls.
